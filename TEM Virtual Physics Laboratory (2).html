Comprehensive Physics Reference Notes

Developed by Sri Kaleeswarar S
Section A: Physics & Relativistic Quantum Mechanics

High-energy electrons moving near the speed of light exhibit relativistic wave dynamics. The total energy $E = \gamma m_0 c^2 = m_0 c^2 + eV$. The relativistic de Broglie wavelength is:

λ = h / √(2 m_0 e V (1 + e V / (2 m_0 c²)))

At $200\text{ kV}$, electrons achieve $\lambda = 2.51\text{ pm} = 0.00251\text{ nm}$ at $v = 0.695 c$, yielding atomic spatial resolution impossible with light optics.

Section B: Image Formation Mechanisms

1. Mass-Thickness Contrast: Heavy elements ($Z$) or thick regions scatter electrons to high angles ($\sigma \propto Z^2$), blocked by the objective aperture to create dark regions in Bright-Field (BF).

2. Diffraction Contrast: Crystalline planes satisfy Bragg's Law ($n\lambda = 2d \sin\theta_B$). Diffracted beams form SAED patterns in the back focal plane.

3. Phase Contrast & PCTF: HRTEM image formation is dictated by interference of transmitted and diffracted waves modulated by the Phase Contrast Transfer Function $\text{PCTF} = \sin(\chi(q))$, where $\chi(q) = \pi \lambda q^2 (\Delta f + \frac{1}{2} C_s \lambda^2 q^2)$. Scherzer defocus yields optimal negative phase contrast.

Section C: Electron-Matter Interactions

Elastic Scattering: Electrostatic deflection by atomic nuclei without kinetic energy loss; dominates diffraction contrast and lattice imaging.

Inelastic Scattering: Energy transfer causing inner-shell ionization (generating Characteristic X-rays measured by EDX) and core electron excitations (measured by Electron Energy Loss Spectroscopy EELS).

Section D: Column Optics & Electromagnetic Lenses

Electromagnetic lenses focus electrons via Lorentz force $\mathbf{F} = q(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B})$. Lens aberrations limit resolution:

Spherical Aberration ($C_s$): Marginal rays focus tighter than axial rays, blurring points into discs of radius $r_s = C_s \alpha^3$.

Chromatic Aberration ($C_c$): Energy spread $\Delta E$ causes focal dispersion $r_c = C_c \alpha \frac{\Delta E}{E}$. Stigmators compensate non-cylindrical magnetic astigmatism.

Section E: Vacuum Engineering & Beam Sources

FEG sources (Schottky & Cold FEG) produce electron beams with brightness $>10^8\text{ A/cm}^2\text{sr}$ and energy spread $<0.3\text{ eV}$ compared to thermionic $LaB_6$. Column requires Ultra-High Vacuum (UHV $< 10^{-7}\text{ Pa}$) to avoid gas scattering and tip contamination.

Section F: Advanced Specimen Preparation Techniques

Specimens must remain $<100\text{ nm}$ thin to prevent multiple scattering. Ultramicrotomy cuts resin-embedded biological samples with diamond knives. Inorganic crystals are prepared via Focused Ion Beam (FIB) $Ga^+$ milling or electropolishing. Heavy metal stains (Uranyl Acetate, $OsO_4$) bind to lipids/proteins to boost Z contrast.

Section G: Modern Innovations in TEM

1. $C_s$ Aberration Correctors: Hexapole/octupole magnetic systems cancel negative spherical aberration, achieving sub-0.5 Å atomic resolution.

2. Direct Electron Detectors (DED) & Cryo-EM: High DQE CMOS detectors capture single electron events at high frame rates, enabling atomic-resolution structure determination of biological macromolecules (2017 Nobel Prize in Chemistry).

3. 4D-STEM: Pixelated DED records 2D diffraction patterns at every 2D probe position for ptychography and internal electric field mapping.
