Crucial note: Ekin is independent of primary beam energy once the core vacancy is created. It depends on atomic energy levels, which makes AES highly useful for chemical identification.

Step 1: Primary Ionization

A high-energy incident electron strikes a tightly bound core electron, ejecting it and creating a core vacancy.

Step 2: Relaxation

An electron from a higher shell falls into the vacancy. The released energy is transferred to another electron.

Step 3: Auger Electron Emission

The transferred energy ejects a second electron. Its kinetic energy carries information about the element and chemical environment.
